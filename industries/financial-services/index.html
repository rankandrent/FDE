    Where agents earn their place
     in financial services work

    Financial services

    KYC refresh and AML alert triage

    Agents gather customer documents, screening hits and transaction history from your KYC platform and case manager, draft the alert narrative and a recommended disposition, and route it to an analyst. Nothing closes without a named reviewer, and every source the agent read is logged.

    Financial services

    Claims intake and first notice of loss

    For insurers, agents read FNOL emails, adjuster notes, photos and policy wordings, populate the claim in Guidewire or your core system, check coverage against the policy schedule and flag subrogation or fraud indicators for the adjuster, who keeps authority over payment.

    Financial services

    Underwriting and credit memo support

    Agents pull financial statements, bureau data and covenant terms into a draft credit memo or underwriting summary, citing the page each figure came from. Underwriters edit and approve; the agent never sets price, limit or decision, so fair lending accountability stays with your people.

    Financial services

    Reconciliation and break investigation

    Agents match ledger, custodian and bank statement records, trace breaks through trade and cash movements across systems, and propose the journal entry or counterparty query. Proposed entries wait in a maker-checker queue, so your existing segregation of duties stays intact.

    Financial services

    Model documentation and validation evidence

    We build the agent alongside its paperwork: purpose, data lineage, prompt and model versions, test sets, known limitations and monitoring thresholds, written so your second line and model validation team can challenge it under your model risk policy.

    Financial services

    Regulatory reporting and examiner evidence

    Agents assemble evidence packs for examiners and internal audit, tie regulatory return inputs back to source systems, and keep an immutable log of every prompt, retrieval, tool call and human decision, retained to match your records schedule.

    Why do AI projects stall
     at banks and insurers?

    Model risk review comes too late

    Teams build a demo, then find validation needs documentation, test evidence and monitoring nobody captured. Rebuilding for review often takes longer than the build.

    Data sits behind strict entitlements

    Customer, transaction and claims data live in core systems with tight access rules, so outside teams wait weeks for extracts that go stale.

    Generative AI guidance is unsettled

    US model risk guidance now leaves generative and agentic AI to each institution’s existing governance, so you have to set your own bar and defend it.

    Vendors hand over black boxes

    A platform that cannot show why it recommended a disposition is hard to defend to an examiner, an auditor or a complaints team.

    Frequently asked questions

    How are AI agents used in financial services?

    AI agents in financial services mostly handle the preparation work around a human decision: gathering KYC documents, drafting AML alert narratives, reading claims files, building credit memo drafts and investigating reconciliation breaks. The agent reads from your systems, proposes an action and logs its reasoning; an analyst, adjuster or underwriter approves it. Fully autonomous decisions on credit, claims payment or suspicious activity reports are rare, and we think they should stay that way until your governance and your supervisors are comfortable with the evidence.

    Does SR 11-7 apply to generative AI and AI agents?

    Not directly any more. SR 26-2, issued by the Federal Reserve, OCC and FDIC in April 2026, replaced SR 11-7 and places generative and agentic AI models outside its scope, with the agencies signalling a separate request for information on AI. That does not remove obligations under BSA/AML, fair lending, UDAAP, privacy or third-party risk rules. We build as if the agent will be validated anyway: documented purpose, test evidence, known limitations and monitoring, so your model risk team can apply its own standard. Confirm the details with your counsel.

    Can AI agents run inside our own cloud or data center?

    Yes, and that is how a forward deployed pod works by default. Our engineers build in your cloud tenancy or data center, use the model endpoints your institution has approved, such as a private Azure OpenAI or Amazon Bedrock deployment or a self-hosted open-weight model, and authenticate through your identity provider. Customer and transaction data does not leave your environment. If your policy requires it, engineers work on your managed devices or virtual desktops.

    Will an AI agent make credit or claims decisions on its own?

    No, not in the systems we build. The agent prepares the file, cites its sources and proposes an outcome; a named person decides. Here is the limitation to plan around: agents can cut the time an analyst spends per case, but in regulated decisions they rarely remove the review step altogether. If your business case depends on taking people out of a review queue entirely, it is likely to disappoint, and we would rather say so during scoping.

    How do you keep an audit trail for AI agent actions?

    We log every prompt, retrieved document, model and prompt version, tool call, output and human decision, with timestamps and user identifiers, into your log store or SIEM under your retention rules. Logs are write-once, so an examiner or internal auditor can replay exactly what the agent saw and proposed on any case. The same records feed monitoring dashboards that show override rates by reviewer and case type.

    How long does it take to get an AI agent into production at a bank?

    For one workflow, plan on roughly 12–16 weeks from kickoff to handover, with production use beginning during a supervised pilot. The code is rarely the critical path. Third-party risk onboarding, data entitlements and validation queues usually are, so we start them in week one. Institutions with long vendor approval cycles or a validation backlog should expect the upper end of that range or longer.
