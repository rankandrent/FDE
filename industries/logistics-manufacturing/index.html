    Where agents fit
     in freight, warehouse and plant operations

    Logistics and manufacturing

    Freight and customs document processing

    Agents extract data from bills of lading, commercial invoices, packing lists and certificates of origin, cross-check them against each other and the booking, suggest HS codes for unclassified lines and stage the entry in your customs system for a licensed broker to file.

    Logistics and manufacturing

    Shipment exception handling

    Agents watch carrier EDI, telematics and TMS milestones for missed pickups, dwell and delays, find the affected orders, draft the customer notification and propose a rebooking. Dispatchers approve the action, and each exception is tagged by cause for carrier scorecards.

    Logistics and manufacturing

    Freight audit and invoice matching

    Agents compare carrier invoices with rate confirmations, contracts and proof of delivery, flag accessorial charges such as detention or lumper fees that lack support, and queue disputes for your freight audit team with the evidence attached.

    Logistics and manufacturing

    Maintenance triage and work orders

    Agents read sensor alerts, operator logs and equipment manuals, suggest a likely cause and procedure, and draft a work order in SAP PM, Maximo or Fiix with parts availability checked. Planners approve, and agents never send commands to control systems.

    Logistics and manufacturing

    ERP, MES and WMS integration

    The pod builds the integrations agents and people both need: order, inventory and production data moving between SAP, Oracle or Dynamics, your MES and your WMS, with reconciliation reports so planners stop rekeying data between screens.

    Logistics and manufacturing

    Supplier and purchase order follow-up

    Agents read supplier acknowledgements, ASNs and email replies, compare promised dates with MRP requirements, chase late confirmations and warn buyers of shortages before they stop a line. Buyers keep authority over expedites, substitutions and price changes.

    Why does operations AI stall
     between pilot and floor?

    Documents arrive in every format

    Scanned, stamped and carrier-specific paperwork defeats template OCR, so pilots tuned on clean samples fail at real volume.

    Systems of record don’t talk

    ERP, TMS, WMS and MES each hold part of the picture, and an agent without access to all of them guesses.

    OT security rules are strict

    Plant networks are segmented for good reason, and any connection near control systems needs review against standards such as IEC 62443.

    Operators need answers in minutes

    A dispatcher or planner will not wait on a slow tool, so latency and clear escalation matter as much as accuracy.

    Frequently asked questions

    How are AI agents used in logistics and manufacturing?

    Mostly on the paperwork and coordination between systems: reading freight and customs documents, working shipment exceptions, auditing carrier invoices, following up with suppliers and turning maintenance alerts into draft work orders. Agents pull data from your ERP, TMS, WMS, MES and email, prepare the next step and ask a person to approve it. They work alongside your planning and control systems rather than replacing them.

    Can AI read bills of lading and customs documents accurately?

    Current models handle varied layouts, stamps and handwriting far better than template OCR, but accuracy still depends on scan quality and how unusual a carrier’s format is. That is why we cross-check fields across the bill of lading, commercial invoice and packing list and route mismatches to a person. For customs, the agent prepares and suggests; a licensed customs broker reviews and files, because classification errors carry penalties the broker answers for.

    Do AI agents connect directly to PLCs or control systems?

    No, and we advise against it. Our agents read operational data through historians, MES or approved gateways and write only to business systems such as your CMMS or ERP. Sending commands to PLCs or SCADA would put a probabilistic model inside a safety-critical loop, which IEC 62443-style network segmentation exists to prevent. If you want closed-loop process optimization, that is a controls engineering project, and an agent build is the wrong tool for it.

    Which ERP, WMS and TMS systems can you integrate with?

    We integrate with common platforms such as SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, NetSuite, Manhattan, Blue Yonder and Maximo, using whatever APIs, EDI feeds, database views or file drops your systems expose. Where an older system has no API, we use your approved integration middleware or, as a last resort, controlled screen automation. We confirm the integration path for each system during scoping, before committing to a timeline.

    How do you handle multiple sites, carriers and languages?

    We start with one lane, warehouse or plant so the agent is tested on real volume, then add sites with a rollout playbook covering new document types, carrier formats, languages and local approval rules. Each addition gets its own test set before going live. Agents can read documents in the major trade languages, but customs and labour rules differ by country, so each new country needs a review with your compliance team.

    How long does it take to deploy an AI agent in logistics operations?

    About 12–16 weeks for the first workflow at one site or lane, including a pilot with human approval on every action. The main variables are integration access, particularly to older ERP or WMS instances, and OT security review when plant data is involved. Expanding to further sites is usually faster because integrations, test sets and approval workflows are reused rather than rebuilt.
