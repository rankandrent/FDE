    Why do AI features stall
     between demo and release?

    Your prototype never met real users

    The demo worked on clean sample data and a single happy path. Production needs auth, error states, latency budgets and a fallback when the model is wrong.

    Your team is booked on the roadmap

    Your engineers own the core product and on-call. An AI feature that touches frontend, backend and data rarely gets a full sprint of their attention.

    Nobody owns prompts, evals and cost

    Model behavior changes with every prompt edit or provider update. Without evals, logging and token budgets, you find regressions through support tickets.

    Vendors build beside your code, not in it

    A separate app or sandbox repo looks fast until you try to merge it. Then your team inherits code that ignores your patterns and pipeline.

    What the full-stack pod builds
     inside your product

    Full-stack FDE pod

    AI feature scoping and design

    We turn one business outcome into a buildable spec: user flows, acceptance criteria, the data the feature needs, model options and a cost estimate per request. You get a one-page decision record before any code is written, so scope stays fixed.

    Full-stack FDE pod

    Backend services and model gateway

    We build the APIs that call your chosen models through a single gateway with retries, timeouts, caching and rate limits. Provider keys stay in your secrets manager, and swapping models later means changing configuration, not rewriting services.

    Full-stack FDE pod

    Retrieval and RAG pipelines

    When the feature needs your documents or records, we build chunking, embedding and retrieval against your vector store or database. Access rules from your source systems carry through, so users only see answers drawn from content they can already open.

    Full-stack FDE pod

    Frontend and streaming UI

    We build the screens in your existing framework and design system: streaming responses, citations, edit and retry controls, and clear states for loading, empty and failed answers. Users see where an answer came from and can correct it.

    Full-stack FDE pod

    Evals, guardrails and human review

    We write an evaluation set from real examples and run it in CI on every prompt or model change. Input filters, output checks and human-in-the-loop review queues catch bad answers before they reach customers or downstream systems.

    Full-stack FDE pod

    Observability and cost controls

    We log prompts, responses, latency and token spend per feature and per tenant, with dashboards and alerts in the tools you already run. You can see which requests cost the most and set budgets before a bill surprises you.

    Full-stack FDE pod

    Tests, CI/CD and staged release

    Code goes through your pull request reviews, test suites and deployment pipeline. We ship behind feature flags, roll out to a small cohort first and keep a documented rollback path for every release the pod makes.

    How the pod gets you
     from idea to production

    01

    1. We review your codebase, architecture and data access, then agree on one measurable outcome with your product owner. We also confirm how the feature fails safely.

      Deliverables: Outcome brief, architecture notes, acceptance criteria, risk log

    2. We ship a working end-to-end slice behind a feature flag in your staging environment. Internal testers try it against an agreed eval set.

      Deliverables: Working slice, eval set, feature flag, demo recording

    3. We add guardrails, monitoring, load tests and cost limits, then roll out in stages. Your engineers review every pull request as it lands.

      Deliverables: Production release, dashboards, runbooks, rollback plan

    4. Your team takes the on-call rotation while we pair on fixes and the next small change. We close with a walkthrough of the code, prompts and evals.

      Deliverables: Handover sessions, documentation, eval suite, next-step backlog

    Frequently asked questions

    What is a full-stack AI development team?

    A full-stack AI development team is a small group of engineers who build an AI feature across every layer: data access, backend services, model calls, user interface, tests and deployment. At tkxel this is a pod of 3–5 engineers that works inside your repository and sprint cadence. Because one team owns every layer, retrieval, the model gateway and the interface are designed together instead of being stitched across separate teams.

    How is an FDE pod different from staff augmentation?

    A pod owns an outcome, while staff augmentation adds individuals you manage. With augmentation, you plan the work, coordinate the specialists and carry the architecture risk. A tkxel pod agrees on one measurable result with your product owner, plans its own work inside your process and is accountable for getting it to production. You still review the code and set priorities, but you do not manage day-to-day tasks.

    What kinds of AI features can the pod build?

    The pod builds features where a model works on your data inside your product, such as copilots, semantic search, document summarization, classification, drafting assistants and agent workflows that call your internal APIs. It also builds the supporting pieces those features need: retrieval pipelines, review queues, logging and admin controls. If the feature depends on training models on large proprietary datasets, we pair the pod with tkxel’s machine learning engineers.

    Will you work in our codebase and tools?

    Yes, the pod works in your repository, ticketing system, CI/CD pipeline and cloud accounts under your access policies. Every change goes through your pull request reviews and your deployment process. tkxel is ISO 27001 certified, and we follow your security requirements for access, secrets and data handling, including working only in approved environments when production data is involved.

    How long does it take to ship an AI feature to production?

    Most single-feature engagements run seven to thirteen weeks from scoping to handover, based on the four phases we plan for. A thin end-to-end slice usually reaches staging within the first month. The timeline stretches when data access approvals, security reviews or unclear acceptance criteria hold up the work, which is why we surface those blockers in the first two weeks.

    When is a full-stack pod the wrong choice?

    A pod is the wrong choice when the work is a simple API call that your own senior engineer can add in a few days. It is also a poor fit if you cannot name one outcome or give the pod access to your codebase, because embedding is how the pod avoids building something you cannot merge. The trade-off is that a pod costs more per week than a single contractor, in exchange for owning delivery end to end.

    What happens after the pod hands over?

    After handover your team owns the feature, including on-call, prompts and the eval suite. During the final phase we pair with your engineers on real fixes, walk through the code and document runbooks for common failures. If you want help after that, you can keep one engineer on a light support arrangement or start a new pod on the next outcome.
