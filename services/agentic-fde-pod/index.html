    Why do so many AI agent pilots
     never reach production?

    The demo works, the workflow does not

    Pilots run on clean sample data. Real records carry exceptions, missing fields, and approvals the prototype never saw.

    Nobody can say how good the agent is

    Without evaluation sets, every prompt change is a guess. Teams stall because they cannot prove the agent is safe to widen.

    Integration is the hard 80%

    Agents need scoped access to ERP, CRM, and document stores. Vendor tools stop at the API boundary your systems actually sit behind.

    The partner leaves, the knowledge leaves

    Consulting teams hand over slides. Your engineers inherit code they did not write and cannot safely change.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Use-case scoping and autonomy design

    We map the workflow step by step and decide where the agent acts alone, where it drafts for review, and where a human must approve. The output is an autonomy matrix your risk team can sign.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Multi-agent system engineering

    Planner, worker, and reviewer agents built on your chosen model and framework, with tool calls, retries, and state handled explicitly. Code lives in your repositories from the first commit.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Evaluation harness and quality gates

    Golden datasets drawn from real cases, automated scoring, and regression checks in CI. Every prompt or model change runs against the same bar before it reaches users.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Enterprise system connectors

    Scoped, audited connections to ERP, CRM, ticketing, and document stores, including MCP servers where they fit. Agents get least-privilege access that security can review line by line.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Guardrails and human-in-the-loop review

    Input and output validation, policy checks, and review queues that route low-confidence actions to people. Reviewers see the agent’s reasoning and sources before approving.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Observability and cost controls

    Tracing for every agent run, dashboards for accuracy and latency, and spend alerts per workflow. You see what each agent did, what it cost, and where it failed.

    Agentic FDE pod

    Handover and team enablement

    Pairing with your engineers throughout, runbooks for every agent, and a staged transfer of ownership. The pod exits when your team can extend the system without us.

    How an agentic FDE pod
     moves from workflow to production

    01

    1. We sit with the people who run the workflow today, collect real cases, and agree on success metrics. We also confirm data access and approval rules before any code is written.

      Deliverables: Workflow map, autonomy matrix, success metrics, evaluation dataset v1

    2. The pod builds one agent end to end inside your environment, wired to real systems and scored against the evaluation set. Your engineers pair on the work from day one.

      Deliverables: Working agent in staging, evaluation report, architecture decision record

    3. We add guardrails, review queues, tracing, and cost alerts, then release to a limited user group. Autonomy widens only when evaluation scores and reviewer feedback support it.

      Deliverables: Production release, monitoring dashboards, incident runbook

    4. Additional agents reuse the same connectors, evaluation harness, and controls. Ownership moves to your team in stages until the pod is no longer needed.

      Deliverables: Agent backlog, ownership transfer plan, team enablement sessions

    Frequently asked questions

    What exactly is a forward deployed engineer?

    A forward deployed engineer works inside the client’s environment rather than from a separate delivery centre. They join your standups, commit to your repositories, and talk directly to the people who run the workflow. The model comes from product companies that embed engineers with customers to make complex software work in real conditions. At tkxel it means a small pod that builds, ships, and then hands over production systems.

    How is an FDE pod different from staff augmentation?

    Staff augmentation adds people to your backlog and you direct the work. An FDE pod owns an outcome, such as a specific agent reaching production, and brings its own delivery method, evaluation tooling, and architecture experience. The pod also plans its own exit, so the engagement ends with your team owning the system rather than depending on extra headcount.

    Which models and frameworks do you use?

    We work with the model and cloud your organisation has approved, including Claude, GPT, and Gemini models through their APIs or through Amazon Bedrock, Google Vertex AI, and Azure. Framework choice follows the workflow; we use established agent frameworks where they help and plain code where they add complexity. Everything is documented so your team can swap components later.

    How large is a pod and how long does it stay?

    A typical pod is three to five people: a lead engineer, one or two AI engineers, and a data or integration engineer, with a delivery lead shared across the engagement. Most first agents reach a limited production release within two to three months. Larger programmes keep the pod longer while ownership transfers in stages.

    Can you guarantee the agent will be fully autonomous?

    No, and we would be cautious of anyone who does. Many workflows include decisions that should stay with a person for regulatory, financial, or customer reasons. We design autonomy per step, release with human review where accuracy is not yet proven, and widen it only when evaluation results support the change. Some steps may stay human-approved permanently, and that is often the right outcome.

    How do you handle our data and security requirements?

    The pod works inside your environment under your access controls, so data does not leave systems you approve. Connectors use least-privilege, audited access, and we align with your security review process from the first week. tkxel holds ISO 27001 certification, and we can work within SOC 2, HIPAA, or industry-specific controls your organisation already runs.

    What do we need to have in place before starting?

    A named business owner for the workflow, access to real examples of the work, and at least one engineer who will eventually own the system. Clean data is helpful but not required; the discovery phase identifies data gaps and whether they block the first release or can be handled with review steps.
