    What can go wrong
     once your AI is live?

    Your chatbot follows the wrong instructions

    A user or a poisoned document tells the model to ignore its rules. It complies, and your system prompt or policy no longer holds.

    Your AI exposes data it shouldn’t

    Retrieval pulls records the user has no right to see, or the model repeats secrets from its context. Nobody notices until a customer reports it.

    Your agent takes actions nobody approved

    An agent with access to email, tickets or a database can be steered into sending, deleting or changing things outside its task.

    Your regular pentest missed all of it

    Standard web and network tests check endpoints and configs. They don’t test how a model reasons over hostile input.

    What we test
     across models, data and agents

    AI penetration testing

    Direct and indirect prompt injection

    We craft inputs that try to override your system prompt, both typed by users and hidden in documents, web pages, emails or tool outputs the model reads. Multi-turn escalation and system prompt extraction are part of every engagement.

    AI penetration testing

    Sensitive data leakage

    We test whether the model reveals personal data, credentials, internal documents or other tenants’ records. That includes retrieval that ignores user permissions, verbose error messages and secrets left in prompts, logs or vector stores.

    AI penetration testing

    Unsafe tool use by agents

    We try to steer agents into calling tools outside their task, chaining tools into unauthorized outcomes or acting on poisoned tool descriptions. We check whether permissions follow least privilege and whether risky actions need human confirmation.

    AI penetration testing

    Jailbreaks and guardrail bypass

    We run role-play, encoding, language-switching and multi-step jailbreak techniques against your content filters and policy layer. Each successful bypass is logged with the exact prompt chain so your team can reproduce and patch it.

    AI penetration testing

    Insecure output handling

    We check what happens to model output downstream: rendered markdown that executes script, generated SQL or code passed to an interpreter, and links that leak data. These turn a model mistake into a classic application vulnerability.

    AI penetration testing

    RAG and data pipeline poisoning

    We test whether an attacker can plant content in the sources your retrieval system indexes and use it to change answers or trigger actions. We also review ingestion controls, document permissions and how sources are cited.

    AI penetration testing

    Model and API abuse

    We probe rate limits, cost controls, authentication and tenant isolation on your model endpoints. That covers denial-of-wallet attacks, key exposure in client code and gaps between your API gateway and the model provider.

    How an AI penetration test runs
     from threat model to retest

    01

    1. We map your AI system: models, prompts, retrieval sources, tools, permissions and data flows. We agree on attacker profiles, test boundaries and rules of engagement with your security lead.

      Deliverables: Threat model, attack surface map, rules of engagement

    2. Our testers run manual and automated attacks across the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications and relevant MITRE ATLAS techniques. Critical findings are reported to you the same day.

      Deliverables: Test logs, reproduction prompts, critical finding alerts

    3. We rank every finding by exploitability and business impact, with proof of concept and a specific fix. We walk your engineers through the report and answer questions.

      Deliverables: Findings report, risk ranking, remediation guidance

    4. Once your team has applied fixes, we rerun the original attacks and variants of them. We close findings only when the attack no longer works.

      Deliverables: Retest report, closure evidence, regression prompt set

    What you gain from testing
     before attackers do

    Close the gaps attackers would find first

    Testers think like an adversary and chain techniques, which surfaces issues a checklist or scanner run misses.

    Prove the fixes actually work

    A retest reruns each attack against your patched system and closes findings only on evidence.

    Answer customer and auditor questions

    Findings map to OWASP and MITRE ATLAS, which gives security reviewers and auditors a recognized reference.

    Keep testing after we leave

    We hand over the regression prompt set, so your team can rerun the attacks after each model or prompt change.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is AI penetration testing?

    AI penetration testing is a security assessment where testers attack your LLM applications, retrieval systems and agents to find exploitable weaknesses before real attackers do. It focuses on risks a traditional pentest does not cover, such as prompt injection, sensitive data leakage, jailbreaks and agents misusing their tools. Findings are ranked by risk, mapped to the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications and MITRE ATLAS, and verified again after your team fixes them.

    What is the difference between AI red teaming and AI penetration testing?

    AI penetration testing checks a defined system for exploitable vulnerabilities within an agreed scope, while AI red teaming simulates a goal-driven adversary across a wider surface. In practice the terms overlap, and many buyers use them interchangeably. Our engagements combine both: a scoped test of your LLM application or agent, plus adversarial scenarios such as getting the agent to send data outside your company, chained across prompts, documents and tools.

    What is prompt injection and how do you test for it?

    Prompt injection is an attack where crafted text makes a model ignore its original instructions and follow the attacker’s instead. Direct injection comes from what a user types; indirect injection hides instructions in content the model reads, such as documents, emails or web pages. We test both, including multi-turn escalation and system prompt extraction, and record the exact prompt chain for every successful attack so your team can reproduce it.

    Do you follow the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications?

    Yes, we use the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications as the baseline checklist and map each finding to it. We also map techniques to MITRE ATLAS, which catalogs adversary tactics against AI systems, and to OWASP’s agentic AI guidance when agents are in scope. The frameworks set minimum coverage, but our testers also build attacks around your specific workflows and permissions, since that is where the most damaging issues usually sit.

    How long does an AI penetration test take?

    A typical AI penetration test takes 2–4 weeks from threat modeling to final report, plus a retest once your fixes are in. A single chatbot with one retrieval source sits at the short end. A multi-agent system with several tools, data sources and user roles needs longer. Critical findings are reported the same day we confirm them, so you do not wait for the final report to start fixing.

    Can a penetration test make our AI system fully secure?

    No, a penetration test cannot make an AI system fully secure; it shows what was exploitable at the time of testing. LLM behavior is probabilistic, and a model update, prompt change or new tool can reopen issues we closed. That is why we hand over a regression prompt set and recommend retesting after major changes. Treat the test as one control alongside input filtering, least-privilege tool access and monitoring.

    Will testing affect our production environment?

    Testing does not have to touch production; we prefer a staging environment that mirrors your production models, prompts, tools and permissions. If only production is available, we agree on rules of engagement first, such as test accounts, rate limits, blocked destructive actions and a named contact who can stop testing. Agents that can send email, move money or delete records are tested with those actions sandboxed or stubbed.
