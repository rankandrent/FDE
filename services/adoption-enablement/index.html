    Why do AI tools
     go unused?

    Generic training, no workflow change

    People learn features in a classroom but never connect them to their own tasks.

    Enthusiasm fades after launch

    Usage spikes in week one and drops once nobody is there to answer questions.

    Staff unsure what is allowed

    Without clear rules, cautious people avoid the tools and risky ones misuse them.

    No measure of progress

    Licence counts show access, not whether anyone works differently.

    How an enablement programme
     rolls out

    01

    1. We measure current AI literacy and tool usage by team and identify the workflows each role spends most time on.

      Deliverables: Literacy baseline, usage report, priority workflows

    2. Role-based sessions run in waves, each built around the priority workflows for that group.

      Deliverables: Session materials, prompt libraries, attendance records

    3. Champions are selected, trained, and coached to support their teams and feed back use cases.

      Deliverables: Champion network, use-case log, coaching notes

    4. Adoption is tracked monthly and training ownership moves to your facilitators or centre of excellence.

      Deliverables: Adoption dashboard, trainer certification, CoE charter

    Frequently asked questions

    Which AI tools does the training cover?

    We train on the tools your organisation has approved, such as Claude, ChatGPT Enterprise, Microsoft Copilot, or Gemini, and on internal assistants we or your team have built. Sessions focus on workflows rather than one product’s features.

    How do you measure adoption?

    We combine three signals: literacy assessments before and after training, actual usage data from the tools, and short pulse surveys on how work has changed. Together they show which teams have adopted and which need more support.

    Can training be delivered on-site?

    Yes. Sessions can run on-site, remote, or hybrid. Executive sessions are usually in person; larger waves often run remotely to reach more teams quickly.

    How many champions do we need?

    A common ratio is one champion for every 20 to 40 people, with at least one in each department. The right number depends on how varied the workflows are across teams.

    Will training alone change how people work?

    Usually not. Training without follow-up tends to fade within weeks. That is why the programme pairs sessions with champions, clear usage guidelines, and monthly measurement. Some teams will still adopt slowly, and the data helps you decide where to focus.

    Do you write our AI usage policy?

    We help draft it with your legal, security, and HR teams, so the training and the policy say the same thing. Final approval stays with your organisation.
