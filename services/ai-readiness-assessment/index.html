    Why do AI plans stall
     before the first release?

    You have ideas but no ranking

    Every team has a favorite AI project, and nobody has compared them on value, feasibility and risk. Budget goes to whoever argues loudest.

    Your data isn’t ready for models

    Records sit in silos with gaps, duplicates and unclear owners. A pilot works on a clean sample, then breaks on production data.

    Leadership wants a business case

    Your board asks what AI will return and when. Vendor demos don’t answer that, and an internal estimate lacks a method.

    Governance questions block approval

    Security, legal and compliance ask who owns model risk, where data flows and how outputs are audited. Without answers, projects wait.

    What the assessment covers
     and what you take away

    AI readiness assessment

    AI maturity scoring

    We score you across strategy, data, technology, governance and people on a five-level scale. Each score links to evidence from interviews and system reviews, so you see why you landed there and what moves you up a level.

    AI readiness assessment

    Process mapping

    We map the workflows where AI could change cost, speed or quality, from intake to decision to hand-off. The maps show volumes, wait times and manual steps, which become the baseline for every business case we write.

    AI readiness assessment

    Data readiness audit

    We profile the sources each use case depends on for completeness, accuracy, access rights and lineage. You get a gap list per use case, with the cleanup, labeling or integration work needed before a model can run on it.

    AI readiness assessment

    Ranked use-case portfolio

    We score candidate use cases on value, feasibility, data readiness and risk, then rank them in one portfolio. Each entry names the owner, the systems involved and the first measurable outcome, so the ranking is easy to defend.

    AI readiness assessment

    Business case per use case

    For the top use cases we build a business case with cost to build, cost to run, expected savings or revenue, and payback period. Assumptions are written down so your finance team can test and adjust them.

    AI readiness assessment

    Technology and architecture review

    We review your cloud setup, integration layer, identity model and existing tools against what the top use cases need. You get a target architecture sketch and a buy, build or extend recommendation for each component.

    AI readiness assessment

    Governance and risk check

    We check how you would govern models in production: data privacy, access control, audit trails, human review points and regulatory exposure such as the EU AI Act. Gaps are listed with a named owner and a fix.

    AI readiness assessment

    Phased AI roadmap

    We turn the ranked portfolio into a roadmap across quick wins, 6–12 month builds and longer bets. Each phase lists dependencies, team needs and a priced first step, so you can start the next quarter without a second study.

    How a 2–4 week assessment
     runs from kickoff to roadmap

    01

    1. We meet your sponsors to agree on business goals, scope and the teams we will interview. We collect existing strategy documents, system inventories and data catalogs before fieldwork starts.

      Deliverables: Kickoff brief, interview plan, document request list

    2. We interview process owners and engineers, map priority workflows and review the systems behind them. We profile the data sources each candidate use case depends on.

      Deliverables: Process maps, system inventory, data profiling results

    3. We score maturity across five dimensions and rank use cases on value, feasibility, data readiness and risk. We build business cases for the top candidates with your finance contacts.

      Deliverables: Maturity scorecard, ranked use-case portfolio, business cases

    4. We turn the ranking into a phased roadmap with dependencies, team needs and costs. We present findings to your leadership and hand over every working file.

      Deliverables: AI roadmap, executive readout, priced first-phase plan

    Frequently asked questions

    What is an AI readiness assessment?

    An AI readiness assessment is a structured review of whether your data, systems, processes, people and governance can support AI in production. At tkxel it runs 2–4 weeks at a fixed price and ends with a maturity score, a data readiness audit, a ranked list of use cases with business cases, and a phased roadmap. The goal is to tell you which AI projects to fund first and what has to be fixed before they can work.

    How long does an AI readiness assessment take?

    Our AI readiness assessment takes 2–4 weeks, depending on how many business units and data sources are in scope. A single department with a few core systems usually fits in two weeks. A multi-region company with several ERPs, CRMs and data platforms needs closer to four. The timeline holds only if your process owners and engineers are available for interviews in the first two weeks, so we schedule those during kickoff.

    What does an AI readiness assessment cost?

    We price each AI readiness assessment as a fixed fee agreed before work starts, based on the number of teams, workflows and data sources in scope. You know the full cost upfront and there are no hourly overruns. Market prices vary widely, and published vendor offers range from a few thousand dollars for a two-week review to tens of thousands for multi-week studies. Ask us for a quote once we have discussed your scope.

    What do we get at the end of the assessment?

    You get five deliverables: a maturity scorecard across strategy, data, technology, governance and people; process maps of your priority workflows; a data readiness audit per use case; a ranked use-case portfolio with business cases for the top candidates; and a phased roadmap with a priced first step. We also hand over the working files, scoring model and interview notes so your team can update the ranking later.

    How do you prioritize AI use cases?

    We prioritize AI use cases by scoring each one on business value, technical feasibility, data readiness and risk, then ranking them in one portfolio. Value comes from the process maps and your finance inputs, such as hours saved or revenue affected. Feasibility and data readiness come from our system review and data profiling. Risk covers privacy, regulatory exposure and the cost of a wrong output. The weights are agreed with your sponsors before scoring starts.

    Is an assessment worth it if we already know our first use case?

    An assessment is still useful if you have a first use case, but you may need a shorter version. If the use case, data and owner are already clear, we can skip portfolio ranking and focus on the data audit, architecture and business case for that one project. The trade-off is that you lose the comparison against other options, so a stronger use case elsewhere in the business can go unnoticed.

    Can tkxel build the use cases after the assessment?

    Yes, tkxel can build the use cases after the assessment through a forward deployed engineering pod of 3–5 engineers. The pod works inside your environment, owns one outcome from the roadmap, ships it to production and hands it over to your team. You are not obliged to use us for delivery, and the roadmap is written so any capable team can pick it up.
