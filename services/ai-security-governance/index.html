    Frequently asked questions

    How is AI penetration testing different from a normal pen test?

    Traditional tests target networks and applications. AI testing adds attacks specific to models and agents, such as prompt injection through documents or emails, extracting confidential data through crafted questions, and tricking agents into misusing their tools. We run both kinds where needed.

    Do you test third-party AI tools or only custom systems?

    Both. For third-party tools we test how they are configured and connected in your environment, which is where most risk sits. For custom systems we also review the code and prompts.

    Which frameworks do you align to?

    We commonly map to NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, and OWASP guidance for LLM applications, alongside your existing ISO 27001 or SOC 2 controls.

    Can testing make an AI system fully secure?

    No. Testing reduces known risks and shows how the system behaves under attack, but new techniques appear regularly. That is why we recommend monitoring and periodic retesting rather than a one-time sign-off.

    Who sits on an AI governance council?

    Typically a senior sponsor, plus leaders from technology, security, legal or compliance, data, and the business units using AI. The council meets on a fixed schedule and decides on use cases, risk thresholds, and incidents.

    How long does a first review take?

    An inventory, permission review, and penetration test for a small set of AI systems usually takes four to six weeks. Larger estates are reviewed in waves, starting with the systems that carry the most risk.
