    Why are signed customers
     still waiting to go live?

    Deals close faster than you onboard

    Sales keeps signing, and every new contract joins a queue of implementations your small team can’t clear. Customers wait weeks before they see value.

    Your engineers keep getting pulled in

    Each custom integration or data migration lands on the product team. Roadmap work slips while they build connectors for one account.

    Hiring a services team is slow and risky

    Recruiting implementation engineers takes months, and demand swings with your sales cycle. You end up overstaffed or behind.

    Stalled onboarding turns into churn

    A customer who never fully launches doesn’t renew. The renewal conversation starts with a project that never finished.

    How a pod joins your team
     and works through the backlog

    01

    1. Engineers go through your product training, implementation playbook and API documentation, then shadow your team on live projects. We agree on branding, tools and escalation paths.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is forward deployed engineering for SaaS companies?

    Forward deployed engineering for SaaS companies means engineers work directly in your customers’ environments to implement, integrate and launch your product, owning the outcome until the customer is live. tkxel offers this as a white-label service: a pod of 3–5 engineers works under your brand, runs implementations, onboarding and integrations, then hands each live account to your customer success team. You get delivery capacity without building an in-house services team.

    Can we outsource customer implementation without losing the relationship?

    Yes, you can outsource customer implementation and keep the relationship if the partner works under your brand. Our engineers use your email domain, project tools and communication templates, and your customers deal with your company throughout. You own the account data, the contract and the renewal. Your customer success manager stays the senior contact, and our engineers report through your implementation lead.

    What does a white-label implementation partner do?

    A white-label implementation partner delivers onboarding, configuration, data migration and integrations for your customers while presenting as part of your company. Unlike a reseller or systems integrator, it does not sell to or own your customers. At tkxel, that means engineers trained on your product who follow your playbook, build connectors through your APIs and close each project with documentation and a handover to your support team.

    How fast can your engineers learn our product?

    Our engineers usually take 1–2 weeks to learn your product well enough to take a first implementation with review from your team. They go through your training, documentation and APIs, then shadow live projects. Full independence on complex accounts takes longer and depends on how well your product and playbook are documented. If most knowledge lives in a few people’s heads, ramp-up is slower and we will ask for their time early.

    Should we hire implementation engineers or outsource?

    Hire in-house when implementation demand is steady and implementation is part of how you differentiate; outsource when demand outpaces hiring or swings with your sales cycle. Many software vendors use a hybrid: a small in-house team owns methodology and key accounts, while a partner pod handles volume, integrations and migrations. The trade-off is that an outsourced team needs good documentation and a clear owner on your side to stay aligned.

    Do forward deployed engineers only work for AI startups?

    No, forward deployed engineers are useful for any software vendor whose customers need integration, data work or configuration before they see value. The model became widely known through enterprise data and AI companies, where products depend on each customer’s data. Vertical SaaS, ERP add-ons, fintech platforms and HR software face the same problem. AI features make it more pressing, because they only work once connected to the customer’s own records.

    How is pricing structured for a white-label pod?

    We price a white-label pod as a monthly or quarterly engagement based on pod size and the mix of skills you need, such as integration, data migration or AI engineering. You can resize the pod at the start of each quarter as your pipeline changes. Some vendors pass the cost through to customers as paid implementation fees; others absorb it to shorten sales cycles. We share a quote once we have reviewed your backlog.
