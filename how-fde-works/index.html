    What makes the model
     different from outsourcing

    How FDE works

    Outcome ownership

    The pod is accountable for one measurable result, such as an agent in production or a pipeline your analysts trust. Hours and headcount are a means, not the deliverable.

    How FDE works

    Work inside your stack

    Engineers commit to your repositories, deploy to your cloud accounts, and follow your access controls from the first week, so nothing needs rebuilding before release.

    How FDE works

    Senior, stable pods

    Each pod is led by an engineer who has shipped AI systems before. The same people stay through delivery instead of rotating across accounts.

    How FDE works

    Evaluation before expansion

    AI systems ship with evaluation sets built from real cases. Autonomy and user access widen only when results support it.

    How FDE works

    Governance designed in

    Autonomy limits, audit trails, and human review are agreed with your risk and security teams before release, backed by ISO 27001 controls.

    How FDE works

    A planned exit

    Handover starts on day one through pairing, runbooks, and staged ownership, so the pod leaves when your team can extend the system alone.

    The five stages
     of every engagement

    01

    1. We map workflows, check data readiness, and rank use cases with your business owners.

      Deliverables: Readiness scorecard, ranked use cases, roadmap

    2. Engineers join your tools, ceremonies, and access controls, paired with your future owners.

      Deliverables: Team charter, access plan, success metrics

    3. The first outcome ships in your environment, scored against evaluation sets drawn from real work.

      Deliverables: Production-ready system, evaluation report

    4. Guardrails, monitoring, and review queues go live with a limited user group first.

      Deliverables: Release plan, dashboards, runbooks

    5. Your engineers take over in stages while the pod steps out or moves to the next outcome.

      Deliverables: Ownership checklist, enablement sessions

    Frequently asked questions

    What is forward deployed engineering?

    Forward deployed engineering is a delivery model in which engineers work inside a client’s environment and own a specific outcome in production. The model was popularised by Palantir and is now used by AI companies and services firms to take AI from pilot to production.

    How is tkxel’s FDE model different from outsourcing?

    Outsourcing moves work to a separate team and location. tkxel’s FDE pods work in your repositories, cloud accounts, and ceremonies, own an agreed outcome, and plan their exit from the start so the capability stays with your team.

    How long does a typical engagement last?

    A first outcome usually takes two to four months from assessment to limited production release. Larger programmes keep a pod longer and move it between outcomes while ownership of finished systems transfers in stages.

    Do we have to start with an assessment?

    No. If you already know the use case and have data access sorted, a pod can start directly with a one to two week scoping phase. The assessment is most useful when priorities are unclear.

    Is FDE more expensive than staff augmentation?

    Often, per person, yes. A pod includes senior engineers, delivery leadership, and evaluation tooling, and it carries delivery risk that augmentation leaves with you. If you only need extra capacity on a well-defined backlog, augmentation is usually the cheaper option.

    Where are tkxel’s engineers located?

    tkxel has offices in the USA, Saudi Arabia, Portugal, and Pakistan. Pods can work on-site, remotely, or in a hybrid arrangement, with core hours aligned to your team.
